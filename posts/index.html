A Short History of Explosives

31 January 2015

This is a short article which was published in Oxford’s student popular science magazine Bang! See it in its illustrated glory here (page 21).

Ask the average ten-year-old what chemists do, and ‘make explosions’ would probably be the first answer. As explosions tend to be inconvenient if you are concerned about the structural integrity of your working environment, most chemists try to avoid them. But what is an explosion?

Simply put, it’s the very rapid expansion or release of gas. Any kind of rapid expansion can cause an explosion, whether from a chemical explosive, the intense heat of a nuclear weapon or from steam, e.g. microwaving an unpierced potato for too long. Explosives, though, are by far the most useful way to generate an explosion no matter if you want to fire a gun, demolish a building, or mine. The power of explosives is down to their chemical structure: one gram of RDX, a modern explosive, produces 24,000 times its own volume of gas.
